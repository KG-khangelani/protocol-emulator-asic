# Physical UART delivery with a faster and slower transmitter

Two public FabricFox v2 boards delivered every valid two-byte batch at three programmed baud rates. All four malformed batches were rejected. At the fastest rate, the pad capture placed consecutive starts 4,254 project clocks apart and the largest observed host-service bound was **112 µs** against **340.32 µs** allowed. The analog time from handoff to pin release remains unmeasured. ## Remote instrument access

At the time of the search, Welland's public status endpoints reported both boards present, reachable and idle. The documented UIO1 route connects FPGA package pin 4, RP2350 GPIO26 and PMOD JB2. The board service exposes the RP2350 and uploaded FPGA logic. Its published wiring and user controls identify no calibrated analog path on the target branch. In all 24 cold loads in this matrix, the weak pull-down and pull-up read `0,0` on a driven low, `1,1` on a driven high and `1,1` after the release command. A logic trace with that last result permits either drive state. LabsLand documents an ADALM2000 scope multiplexed among four DE1-SoC FPGAs. Its kit specification omits the target branch, known high/low/released controls and probe calibration required to time this handoff. Another Tiny Tapeout project documents a Keysight scope on a demo board. Both probes go to dedicated outputs `uo[0]` and `uo[1]` for a signal-generator frequency check. SIGPT lists JTAG, UART, power, a camera and on-chip debugging. The search identified no accessible fixture meeting the electrical test. ## Measured digital outcomes

| Programmed peer rate | Rate offset | Start-to-start interval on good pairs | Largest host bound, board 1 / board 4 | Service limit | Good / bad-stop trials across both boards |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 29,384.11 baud | +2.0216% | 4,254 project clocks | 112 / 111 µs | 340.32 µs | 8 delivered / 2 rejected |
| 28,801.84 baud | nominal | 4,340 project clocks | 112 / 113 µs | 347.20 µs | 6 delivered / 0 injected |
| 28,223.08 baud | −2.0095% | 4,429 project clocks | 113 / 112 µs | 354.32 µs | 6 delivered / 2 rejected |

Each board received `55 AA` at all nine combinations of rate and launch delay. A separate fast trial delivered `A5 3C`. Each valid batch ended at `A7`. A fast bad first stop and a slow bad second stop each ended at `E6` without delivery. The second-stop fault discarded a tentative `55`. Before each case the controller read back the 32-byte program, length and both payload registers. A deliberately damaged second program byte was caught on loader edge 38 on both boards. The tests used the E0051 bitstream with SHA-256 `89fa89607e0eac4468d11257b4b46296f9122115e0995c888274d31387fb4163`. [Board 1 receipt](board-1/result.json.gz) · [Board 4 receipt](board-4/result.json.gz)

Three RP2350 PIO state machines supplied the project clock, transmitted the frames and sampled the GPIO26 input buffer. DMA retained **46,080 digital samples per case**, starting from a trigger on the first GPIO16 rising edge. The first UART falling edges occurred at zero-based sample indices **82, 84 and 86** at every rate on both boards. Their separation was two capture intervals, nominally 32 ns. The offline decoder compared all **420 transitions** against the independently constructed frame schedules and decoded the data and stop bits at their centers. The largest observed relative offset was one controller tick with the odd-length cell. The sampling interval is two ticks, nominally 16 ns. These timings refer to the digital input buffer. [Board 1 raw terminal and packed samples](board-1/terminal.bin.gz) · [Board 4 raw terminal and packed samples](board-4/terminal.bin.gz) · [Digital decoder](../../experiments/remote_uart/margin_check.py)

The physical trial deliberately changed the transmitter bit period while keeping the receiver's eight-word program and 12.5 MHz clock fixed. Each bit occupied 4,254, 4,340 or 4,429 controller ticks. Each project clock occupied ten ticks. The digital capture verified the interval between frame starts. Under the council's rearm assumptions the allowed first-byte service time equals ten source bit periods. The register-overwrite edge itself was not timestamped in this run. The host bound adds the largest observed poll gap, the largest observed read sequence and 2 µs for timer quantization. Counting 100,000 project-clock periods at GPIO16 took 8,017–8,040 µs including software observation overhead. All three state machines share the same oscillator. This experiment covers three rate points, three programmed launch delays and two payload pairs. Absolute baud accuracy, independent oscillator drift, analog release latency and ASIC silicon remain open. ## Verification and reproduction

The two matrix runs took place on 24 November 2026 at 22:09:01–22:09:25 UTC and 22:09:56–22:10:20 UTC (25 November in South Africa). The original six-case delivery suite was then run through its default entry point on board 1 at 22:10:42–22:11:03 UTC. It passed with a largest target-rate bound of 109 µs. Each session restored its original controller frequency and returned host bidirectional output enable to zero. Both stable release tags match all twenty qualified physical inputs. The locked Docker environment passed `make doctor` and `make check`, including 163 unit tests.
