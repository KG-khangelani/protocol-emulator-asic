# 3.3 V endpoint and contention measurements before and after silicon

**Equipment status.** This is a proposed fixture. The council identified public specifications and computed its discrimination limits. It did not establish access to the boards or instruments. Before silicon, the fixture can validate the requester schedule, discovery of known-good/failing handovers, receiver thresholds and behavior of an FPGA port of the VM. A 2027 chip/carrier measurement is still needed for its IO cell, mux propagation, board parasitics and whole-system startup.

## Fixture and calibration

Use a Digilent Arty A7 Rev. E.0 with 3.3 V LVCMOS FPGA IO. JA1–4 serve as SCK, MOSI, CS_A and MISO. The Rev. E.0 schematic shows 200 Ω series resistors on all four, including R13 on JA4. A separate 3.3 V Raspberry Pi Pico RP2040 controls the transaction using GP2 for SCK, GP3 for MOSI, GP5 for CS_A and GP4 for returned MISO. GP6 can select a separate 25LC256 EEPROM for read-status-only testing. The CS_B phase is independently scheduled. Tie the signal grounds at the fixture, check each supply/pin voltage before attachment, and avoid parallel USB regulator outputs. Use current-limited dummy drivers for faults. Never oppose two unprotected powered outputs. [Arty schematic, sheet 1](https://digilent.com/reference/_media/reference/programmable-logic/arty-a7/arty_a7_sch.pdf#page=1) · [Pico pinout, Fig. 2](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/pico/pico-datasheet.pdf) · [25LC256 command set and electrical limits](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/MPD/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/25AA256-25LC256-256K-SPI-Bus-Serial-EEPROM-20001822H.pdf)

1. Use two independent 3.3 V GPIO sources and known tri-state controls to calibrate the fixture before loading any FPGA image. A suitable proposed instrument is a Tektronix MSO24 configured to 200 MHz, four active channels at 1.25 GS/s, using compensated TPP0200 10× probes with short grounds. The listed tip capacitance is under 12 pF, the sample period is 0.8 ns and analog vertical resolution is eight bits. Record the actual bandwidth, timebase, probe IDs, reference voltage, cable and loaded rise/fall. Warm up the scope and run its signal-path compensation. Apply matched DC levels and the same edge to both probe tips, swap channels, and measure worst differential zero and crossing skew. Proposed acceptance for the reported timing/current bands is differential uncertainty at most 40 mV and timing uncertainty at most 5 ns including measured skew, threshold uncertainty, sampling and repeatability. At a slow edge, convert voltage uncertainty into time using that trace's local slope. A correction outside these limits leaves that measurement indeterminate. [Tektronix 2 Series MSO specifications, pp. 14–15](https://download.tek.com/datasheet/2SseriesMSO-Datasheet-48W738576.pdf)
2. Put a measured 470 Ω sense resistor between the target header and bus. For the dummy tests, a 1.00 kΩ source/resistor on the bus provides separately repeatable high and low bias. Probe both sides of the sense resistor, plus CS at the target. Use the fourth channel for SCK or the reference/permission signal in matched repeated captures. The current is the measured differential voltage divided by actual resistance. With 1% external resistors, 3.4 V maximum and those paths as the only active sources, even neglecting the Arty series resistor gives a 2.337 mA conservative ceiling on static opposed current. This bound excludes capacitive transients. Choose GPIO ratings that cover the measured network. Use the opposed-bias capture only to discriminate branch ownership. With a nominal active-high target against low bias, the ideal bus node is 1.976 V. With an active-low target against high bias, it is 1.324 V. Neither is a guaranteed corresponding Pico input level. Disconnect the opposing bias for normal byte/setup/hold captures, use the real receiver load, and compare the timed result with and without the series sense fixture. Remove deliberate opposing drives before attaching a commercial target. Validate known continuously high/low, released and intentionally delayed-release states in *both* polarities. At the nominal divider a driven opposite output gives 1.976 mA. An equal-valued simultaneous driver can give zero. [Circuit assumptions and estimates](results/fixture-sensitivity.json)
3. Define a strong-drive discriminator at 0.25 mA, corresponding to 117.5 mV across 470 Ω. For a settled sample, take a conservative uncertainty bound of `U_I = U_ΔV/R_min + abs(I_meas) × U_R/R_min`, using the measured absolute resistance uncertainty. The known driven controls must exceed 1.5 mA after uncertainty and the known released controls must fall below 0.25 mA after uncertainty. Evaluate both source directions. These thresholds can distinguish a strong driver in this jig. Qualifying microamp-level off-state leakage would need a separate calibrated DC measurement and a device-specific requirement.
4. Measure how long the known high-to-Z and low-to-Z controls take to cross this current band with all intended probes and loads attached. An independent two-node RC calculation, using a 200 Ω upstream source and the nominal external resistors, reaches 0.25 mA after **42.899 ns** at 12/12 pF, **89.002 ns** at 24/40 pF, and **214.496 ns** at 60/60 pF, even when the driver turns off instantaneously. At 24/40 pF the predicted current at 160 ns is 0.089 mA for immediate release and 0.285 mA for an 80 ns release. A 60/60 pF fixture can hide timely turnoff. Use smaller-capacitance probes or an independently bounded calibration response when the fixture dominates. A late crossing alone cannot distinguish true late turnoff from probe/jig discharge. The model was checked against a separate RK4 integrator over 160 ns; actual capacitances and driver impedance must be measured. ## Acceptance at the named endpoints

| Priority | Captures and decision |
|---|---|
| 1. Detect ownership and incorrect service | With external targets isolated, load/read back at a held-low project clock and apply E7 → 67 → 00. Check MCU drivers, switch permission, selector, clock and observed lines; repeat deliberate early attaches only with the current-limited dummy. On an ETR Rev. 3.3 fixture, verify J15 and actual firmware routing first. A detector that passes a known continuous-opposite hold, or suggests that an equal-value capture proves two drivers are independent, fails calibration. |
| 2. Qualify the application’s two-frame request | Use a hardware-timed 0.5/1/2 MHz mode-0 requester. Capture actual clocks against a 50 MHz project reference, including a stalled-high SCK abort, return-to-idle 80 ns later, CS reselection at 160 ns and first new SCK rise at +300 ns. Sweep 80/160/320/2,000 ns CS-high intervals with labels for stronger or weaker conditions. Independently log A5 → A7 and A4 → 3C, command-phase MISO release, setup ≥60 ns and hold ≥40 ns at the requester, and absence of stale output or wrong frame. In the declared 160 ns test, the conservative upper bound on strong-drive release must be within 160 ns in both directions. Reject for persisting opposite current after accounting for the upper calibrated tail, or for any wrong bit/frame. Classify an overlapping measurement interval as undecidable. A calibrated CS-high-only pass earns only that assertion. The Pico's guaranteed input states at 3.3 V are high ≥2.0 V and low ≤0.8 V. A 25LC256 companion specifies its own output disable ≤80 ns at 3.3 V, with a periodically sampled datasheet parameter. This peer can be tested with read-only RDSR separately. [RP2040 §5.5.3.4, Table 646](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf#page=592) · [25LC256 Tables 1-1 and 1-2](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/MPD/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/25AA256-25LC256-256K-SPI-Bus-Serial-EEPROM-20001822H.pdf#page=2) |
| 3. Decide whether a firmware retime is needed | Reconfigure for a genuine open-drain I²C bus with measured pull-ups/load. Probe SCL/SDA together at the named receiver, then repeat at the controller end. At 3.3 V, Table 11's 0.3/0.7 VDD thresholds are 0.99/2.31 V. Require a positive uncertainty-adjusted margin from SCL crossing below 0.99 V to a falling SDA leaving 2.31 V, plus Standard-mode rise ≤1 µs, fall ≤300 ns, low ≥4.7 µs, high ≥4.0 µs, setup ≥250 ns and data/ACK validity within 3.45 µs where the Table 11 conditions apply. Preserve successful clock stretch and compare identical named frames for the 96- and 108-word schedules. A separable violation here promotes the 24-clock guard; a byte-only match cannot do so. [NXP UM10204 Rev. 7, §6.1 Table 11, Fig. 38 and notes](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/user-guide/UM10204.pdf#page=44) |
| 4. Validate a one-wire load claim | Place a known locally powered and a known parasite-powered DS18B20 in separate runs, capture DQ at controller and sensor, and independently decode every command edge. Screen the named pull-up/cable/probe combinations, 15/30/60 µs programmable write sampling, and late legal presence relative to sensor-end recognition of reset release. Count 17 slot starts and check the documented reset/command sequence and known power result. A wrong sensor classification or a missing slot rejects that load. Both endpoints' threshold bands and the discharged/idle line must support the result. [DS18B20 Rev. 6, pp. 2–3 and 15–17](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ds18b20.pdf) |

Budget **2–4 engineer-hours** to assemble/calibrate an already available fixture and **3–6 hours** to capture the two-frame control/failure matrix. Allow **0.5–2 engineer-days** if the FPGA wrapper, observability and equivalent synchronous BRAM mapping must first be implemented and regressed. An additional equipped bench-day is a reasonable estimate for the I²C and one-wire sweeps. Actual equipment access and silicon lead time are unknown. A 20 µs four-channel capture at 1.25 GS/s contains roughly 100 kB of 8-bit samples before native-format overhead. Retain the raw first failure, representative calibrated controls and an index of the phase/voltage outcomes. No live run, purchase or equipment booking occurred.
