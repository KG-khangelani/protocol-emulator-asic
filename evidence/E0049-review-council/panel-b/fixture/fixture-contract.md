# 3.3 V fixture discrimination study, 24 November 2026

At 15:50 UTC, before running this calculation, panel B declared a two-node first-order electrical model for a *proposed* current-sensing jig. The purpose is to decide when the instrumented fixture can distinguish released and driven MISO in a 160 ns shared-SPI test. It is independent of the existing C RTL/netlist abort witness. No board or instruments are present in the evidence.

- One surrogate target branch uses 200 Ω on the Arty A7 JA output, an external interchangeable 470 Ω current-sense resistor, a 1.00 kΩ source to the opposite voltage on the bus, and one capacitor to ground at each of the two probed nodes. The two external resistors have assumed 1% tolerances. The source on-state resistance for the nominal model is zero in addition to the board's 200 Ω. Test both bias polarities and the same-level invisibility control.
- Solve the two-node linear discharge from its exact settled divider initial condition. Report the time after ideal driver release when absolute current drops and stays below 0.25 mA, current at 160 ns, and the equivalent remote bus threshold crossing at 0.3/0.7 V of supply. Verify solution against an independent fourth-order integration for 24/40 pF, both polarities and the no-release control. Test 12/12, 24/40, 60/60 and 120/120 pF. These are hypotheses, not attached-probe capacitance measurements.
- Vary an imposed driver-release delay among 0, 80, 160 and 1,563 ns. Repeat at 3.3 V nominal and report a 3.4 V worst-case source/bias resistor bound. Expect prompt release to be distinguishable at lighter loads, heavily loaded measurements to require calibration and a 1,563 ns persistent opposite drive to be rejected. Same-polarity pull alone cannot distinguish a driven output from Z.
- A future physical screen must verify the test network with known high, low, high-Z and deliberately retained opposite-drive conditions. Bound each skew, common-mode differential error, timing sample uncertainty and load with the actual scope. A simulated calculation cannot award any hardware pass.

Before solving, also report Pico RP2040 receiver guaranteed 2.0 V high and 0.8 V low crossings, distinct from the 0.7/0.3 VDD normalized thresholds of the named Microchip 25LC256 inputs.
