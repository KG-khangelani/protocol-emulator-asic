# Reply on ETR observability and the zero-margin PIO case

24 November 2026, 16:34 UTC. The independent ballot stands.

**What to capture.** ETR Rev. 3.3 presents one resolved voltage on each shared J15 UIO signal. Its RP2350 has a readable, commanded GPIO direction mask. An external isolation switch or peer can expose its enable on a separate fixture test point. Neither signal is the ASIC's internal `uio_oe`, which the carrier routes to the pad and does not export separately on J15. A testbench or instrumented FPGA can expose that internal signal as an additional observation. On stock ETR, record the host register and accessible external enable, then infer strong-drive release from calibrated, opposing-bias measurements on both ends of a sense resistor. Two matching push-pull drivers can give the correct voltage and negligible inter-driver current. Even the two-bias experiment establishes only the declared strong-drive threshold and load. Replace “both OE controls” with the actual signal names and identify each as commanded, directly probed or inferred. **What the 160 ns tie means.** At the same reachable internal sampling phase, write the connector interval as the modeled raw-CS-to-OE interval plus the signed difference between the external and modeled endpoint timestamps. For the usual reference points this correction includes CS travel inward and MISO release outward. Input delay can also move the event across a sampling edge, so evaluate that dependence before adding path bounds. At a modeled 160 ns tie, a strictly positive *lower* bound on the correction, after uncertainty, proves a miss for that phase. A positive *upper* bound leaves a possible miss and proves neither pass nor failure. Certification requires an upper bound on the complete connector interval of at most 160 ns across the stated phases and loads, or a justified restriction that excludes the boundary cases. An observed range that straddles 160 ns is unresolved. The FAQ's approximately 10 ns is an unmeasured Tiny Tapeout pad/mux round-trip estimate. It supplies neither a minimum nor maximum for these paths, and describes a different platform from the RP2040 PIO model. **What can go in the competition packet.** Submit the public RTL, executable tests, passing Tiny Tapeout GDS/precheck, documented ISA/toolchain, runnable protocol examples, verification results and AI-use disclosure under the published rules. Show the G4/G5 identities and architectural/functional limits. The one-bit PIO simulation is legitimate comparative evidence when the tie, both stopped-high failures and its narrower workload travel with it. A routed timing report, an instrumented FPGA and the ETR predecessor can each be credited within their own scope. No physical 160 ns guarantee or final-chip measurement is required by the posted packet requirements. The final submission process still needs the organizer's form.
