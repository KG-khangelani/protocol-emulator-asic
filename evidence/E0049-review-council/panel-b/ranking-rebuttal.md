# Electrical rebuttal of the PIO comparison and revised priorities

The PIO experiment gives a second programmable implementation of the same one-target DS18B20 power query. Its 32 × 16-bit instructions, one state machine and preloaded command word fit the job. The VM uses 85 × 32-bit words. On eight positive corners, two target failures and two software mutations, the accepted frame timing and expected rejections agree with the shared digital contract. Aligning to the first DQ drive removes a 49-system-edge launch difference; the terminal offset differs by one system edge on positives. The 115 PIO prelaunch operations and 712 VM clocked loader steps use different host interfaces and time units. The PIO also uses a fourth GPIO for completion, where the VM exposes HALT on its existing dedicated status bus. These observations substantiate schedule expressivity and code footprint. They do not measure relative area, energy, physical bus margin or time to initialize a real host.

**Challenge to architecture.** Both decoders accept command bits by reading a resolved ideal wire at 30 µs. The sensor can sample 15–60 µs into a write slot. Its presence delay starts when the sensor detects the rising DQ edge after reset. The models instead time it from the master's digital release. Both fixtures make a parasite zero low from 10 to 45 µs and classify near 14 µs; the real device and receiving pad have threshold and transport delays. The PIO harness advances a modeled synchronizer and instruction engine itself, with a 50 MHz system clock divided by 50. Its GPIO pad setup selects 4 mA nominal drive, Schmitt input and slow slew. An actual clock of 125 MHz with that same divider would cut a commanded 500 µs reset to 200 µs, below the 480 µs minimum. A hardware comparison must measure the PIO state-machine clock or configure the divider from the actual clock. [DS18B20 Rev 6, electrical limits and time slots](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ds18b20.pdf#page=15) · [RP2040, PIO clocking and GPIO electrical characteristics](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf)

**A counterexample to a physical guarantee.** At 3.3 V the sensor's guaranteed input-high level is 2.2 V on local power and 3.0 V on parasite power. Its own thresholds differ from the RP2040 input specification of 2.0 V minimum for logic high at 3.3 V. With an illustrative 4.7 kΩ pull-up and 1 nF effective load starting at 0 V, the time to 3.0 V is 11.270 µs. At the sensor's earliest 15 µs write-one sampling instant, 9 µs after a 6 µs drive pulse releases, DQ reaches only 2.814 V. A parasite-powered sensor may read high there, but the published high-level specification gives no guarantee. With a reset receiver threshold at 3.0 V and legal 60 µs presence delay, low may begin 71.270 µs after the master released, beyond the programs’ approximately 70 µs check. A 100 pF control reaches 3.0 V in 1.127 µs. This calculation represents one passive rising-edge model. The actual device threshold, leakage, cable, input pad, voltage, supply and probe load were unmeasured. [DS18B20 DC and AC characteristics](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ds18b20.pdf#page=2) · [RP2040 GPIO input characteristics, Table 646](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/rp2040/rp2040-datasheet.pdf#page=592)

**Discriminating bench.** Use the same sensor, supply, external pull-up, cable and calibrated probes for each controller. Independently sweep sensor write sampling at 15, 30 and 60 µs with a programmable target; sweep presence timing relative to the measured sensor-end threshold and read response around the master's actual sample. Label deliberately late responses separately from manufacturer-valid behavior. Then attach a genuine locally powered sensor and one parasite-powered sensor in separate trials, verify the expected power bit independently, and measure DQ at both ends. Include missing/after-reset stuck and prelaunch stuck, terminal stability, clock/launch offset, all command bits, voltage margins and bus recovery. A single device misclassified at a declared load rejects that operating envelope. A loss of guaranteed-high margin limits the claim even when one specimen returned the expected bit. Reading power supply requires no temperature conversion. Parasite conversion, strong-pull-up delivery, multidrop address selection and reliable temperature measurement need other tests. The same FPGA/board setup checks controller timing before fabrication; final CMOS5L analog behavior depends on the actual die. Allow half a day for the digital receiver-phase sweep and one equipped bench-day if the boards, target and instrument are already available.

My original concern that selected-host launch had been demonstrated only for a short idle prefix is superseded for those 55 G3 netlist cases. My dissent about physical ownership, remote pad levels, target scheduling and host service remains. The PIO control strengthens the alternative's programmable timing case and makes a claim of substrate minimality harder to defend. It does not shift priority to a replacement ASIC architecture.
