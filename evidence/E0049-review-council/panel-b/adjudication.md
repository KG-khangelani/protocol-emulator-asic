**Strongest dissent and reversal.** A team with a named requester that selects two targets back-to-back, requires high impedance by 160 ns after either stopped-clock polarity and cannot authorize host reload should allocate its next architecture day to exactly that service contract. The 59-word VM test device delivered both eight-bit replies in complete frames but took 1,563 ns to release on the stopped-high abort. An ideal bilateral switch disconnected while CS was high, reconnected stale MISO and failed to deliver the second 3C. The one-bit, 19-word PIO shows that autonomous reselection is plausible. Its 480 stopped-low sequences included four 160 ns boundaries at each abort position, leaving zero worst-case model allowance for the physical pad. The two stopped-high cases released at 700 and 718 ns after SCK eventually fell. Those figures describe different workloads and do not rank the two cores for an eight-bit target.

The priority reverses from the cold profile or fixture characterization to architecture work when the actual requester trace shows a second selection before the old target can revoke its drive and the delayed device fails the distinct second response under the customer's specified interval. The acceptance trace for an alternative must show release by the deadline after both clock polarities, correct 3C after the aborted A5 without host intervention, command-phase Z, and at least 60 ns setup/40 ns hold at the receiver where the declared test requires it. A second protected bus and a wait-for-ready host can remove the need for native interruption only if that requester accepts the additional scheduling constraint.
