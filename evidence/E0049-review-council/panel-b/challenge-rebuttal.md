# Board ownership and I²C timing debate

Panel B challenged panel C's selected-project launch result, and panel A challenged B's proposed I²C guard. The responses changed both the recommendation and the effort estimate.

## Can an always-selected host safely launch the release?

**Hardware and embedded challenge to software.** The data direction changes when the selector changes, even with the core clock parked. On ETR Rev 3.3 the RP2350 and external J15.2 share UIO1. The UART RX program's second instruction *byte* in word zero is `00`. A read at selector `81` makes the ASIC drive UIO1 low. An attached idle-high push-pull UART transmitter would drive against it throughout that read. Software that parks the clock, verifies bytes and sends `E7` at the end cannot undo that earlier conflict. A final byte/status check can also miss a same-level overlap, an unintended SCL pulse or a UART frame that started outside its arming window. [ETR Rev 3.3 netlist in board CAD](https://github.com/TinyTapeout/tt-demo-pcb/blob/bbf3dd1f771d5aed249b07ddf130b3ddc8261d60/tinytapeout-demo.kicad_pcb) · [Released UART program](https://github.com/KG-khangelani/protocol-emulator-asic/blob/5bdc0e6b6449a829a5da1f8f9a8060257e974843/programs/od128-sram-v1/uart_rx.asm)

**Software rebuttal.** C narrowed its 4–8 hour estimate to a digital host profile and smoke test, then budgeted 1–2 engineer-days plus compute for representative complete peer-driven transactions in two simulators and the actual G4 netlist. A fixture, independent peer isolation, deterministically triggered UART start and worst-case service measurement add separate work. C's later G4/RTL experiment found 360 of 720 monotone bit-clear orders from `E7` to `00` entered a loader read. A single UI7-first step to `67`, followed by `00`, stayed released. Those enumerated intermediate states model deliberately non-atomic changes. Their frequency in the enumeration is not a probability for an MCU.

**Resolution.** Qualify the logical handoff first. Isolate the external partner before accessing any loader register. At parked clock, release all RP2 shared drivers before entering a loader read. Return to a chip-released loader write and allow both pin banks to settle before enabling RP2 drivers. Establish `E7`, release the host, reconnect settled idle, clock twice and stage `E7 → 67 → 00` before execution. During a physical test record clock, selector, independent isolation enable and RP2 OE, then test release with calibrated weak pulls/current-limited observation. A single pin voltage cannot independently identify every driver; internal ASIC OE is only available in the digital test. A peer that cannot be disconnected, guaranteed high impedance or armed on time disqualifies that fixture. [Pinned RP2350 bank writes](https://github.com/TinyTapeout/tt-micropython-firmware/blob/d485c7a9986336024e35d790568fca33be1c5665/src/ttboard/util/platform/rp2350.py#L67-L119)

## Is an I²C hold-time guard necessary, and what does it cost?

**Architecture challenge.** A 20 ns gap between core commands can admit an unfavorable threshold ordering under assumed loads. A asked for the receiver, pad and load that would require firmware to change, the largest guard compatible with the contract, and its priority against an internal hold path.

**Evidence against prescribing a universal guard.** The earlier 0p4 CMOS5L carrier maps UIO0/1 to 30 mA bidirectional pad cells. The foundry-submission netlist contains both instances. G4's pinned PDK has three-state-enable-to-falling-pad Liberty tables at 2, 6, 12, 18, 28 and 40 pF, with input transition grid 0.02–2.5 ns. Across 42 tabulated points per corner the 90–10% output falls span 0.352–3.255 ns fast, 0.508–4.599 ns typical and 0.831–7.252 ns slow. The previously proposed 100/20 ns SCL/SDA falls refer to different, illustrative bus conditions. The official tables use 50% delay and 90–10% slew. They supply no 100 pF bus simulation. They also omit the project's clock-to-OE path, final carrier mux, board loading and remote receiver. The final competition carrier remains to be identified. [Carrier mapping](https://github.com/TinyTapeout/tt-multiplexer/blob/3e496494ee9f543ec4b84e5e71bdaee25e958ce1/ol2/tt_top/tt_ihp_wrapper.v#L84-L91) · [Foundry netlist, instances 32/33](https://github.com/TinyTapeout/tinytapeout-ihp-0p4-foundry-submission/blob/66fb0eb377f4e565a6f7b275ad748bc21783f57a/release/v.1.0.0/netlist/tt_ihp_wrapper.v#L6981-L6996) · [G4-pinned typical OE arc](https://github.com/IHP-GmbH/IHP-Open-PDK/blob/89d42ee6d2fd9b50b86a1aada6fee6673c3e6008/ihp-sg13cmos5l/libs.ref/sg13cmos5l_io/lib/sg13cmos5l_io_typ_1p2V_3p3V_25C.lib#L1676-L1732)

**New digital result.** The exact E0047 combined transaction addressed `0x2e`, wrote `0x96` and received `0x01`. The target reacted 3.45 µs after falling SCL. A fresh 50 MHz Icarus run on the unchanged production RTL and real digital SRAM, with public loading and this interactive ideal target, reproduced the official netlist run's entire normalized trace SHA-256. Inserting one WAIT after each of twelve SCL-low SET sites takes the program from 96 to 108 of 128 words. With the following low dwell held fixed, eight added clocks give 180–200 ns for all 17 changed controller-driven data edges, 97.087 kHz fastest clock and 409.00 µs to HALT. Twenty-four clocks give 500–520 ns, 94.162 kHz and 421.16 µs. The baseline gave 20–40 ns, 98.619 kHz and 402.92 µs. A 200-clock control still read the expected byte and halted but violated the oracle's 3.45 µs data-valid bound.

Reducing each 260-count low dwell by the added guard length preserved all 76 SCL transitions, both START conditions, STOP and HALT in the archived transaction. The fastest clock stayed 98.619 kHz, minimum LOW/HIGH 5.24/4.90 µs and minimum setup 1.81 µs. The table shows the ideal replay and a separate projection for the *earliest controller-driven falling SDA*. Each offline model assumes two independent monotone exponential falls from full supply to zero. It uses SCL crossing 30% before falling SDA crosses 70%. A rising data edge enters the region at 30%. The simulator continued to sample ideal levels.

The adjacent 170/171 observations bracket the digital valid-delay limit for this one transaction and schedule. External voltage transitions consume additional time. The 24-clock retimed program also passed NACK at all three applicable boundaries plus four SCL stretch behaviors at each of 38 releases. Both allowed last observations succeeded and both late/permanent conditions timed out, with all 76 timeout cases halting two clocks after the deadline. The retimed screen contained 159 cases, one simulator and one address/data combination on the historical macro loader. Real target timing, other data, selected-host operation, a second simulator and submitted netlist remain separate gates. Arbitration and multiple controllers have no acceptance contract. **Discriminating fixture.** Use a single-controller ETR Rev 3.3 hookup with an independently enabled Raspberry Pi Pico/RP2040 target, Pico GP4/pin 6 to J15.2 SDA, GP5/pin 7 to J15.1 SCL and common ground. Configure address `0x2e` to record `0x96` and return `0x01` on repeated START. Freeze the target firmware, external 3.3 V pull-ups, added and total capacitance, cable and probes. Measure both receiving endpoints with two deskewed analog channels, declare uncertainty, rail, temperature and load. Measure SDA in both directions at data and ACK cells, transition time, VOL, SCL duty/period, valid/setup, external stretch and terminal outcomes. Let m be the time when SDA enters the indeterminate region minus the time when SCL reaches 30%, and let U be the timing uncertainty. Retain the unguarded operating point only if m − U > 0 and every other limit passes. A reproducible m + U ≤ 0 calls for a guard or narrower load envelope. Intervals spanning zero need better measurement. The target's ISR can stretch the bus; measure it separately. The ideal-case 3.45 µs response represented an instantaneous bus-level change. Adding physical settling to a drive onset at 3.45 µs does not establish a compliant target. [Pico board pinout](https://datasheets.raspberrypi.com/pico/pico-datasheet.pdf) · [Raspberry Pi target API and ISR behavior](https://www.raspberrypi.com/documentation/pico-sdk/high_level.html#pico_i2c_slave)

For an unmeasured illustrative 4.7 kΩ pull-up and 400 pF load, `0.8473 RC` gives 1.59 µs rise, above the 1 µs Standard-mode limit. A fall guard cannot correct that rise. An FPGA on the ETR can establish host sequencing and partner behavior before fabrication. The eventual ASIC pad and package require the produced part or separately justified pad-level characterization. Allow 0.5–1 day for a bench already equipped with a controllable target and calibrated scope. An adverse fixture result promotes targeted firmware verification. A confident in-envelope pass leaves the qualified bytes in place. [NXP pull-up sizing](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/user-guide/UM10204.pdf#page=50) · [Official FPGA breakout](https://tinytapeout.com/guides/fpga-breakout/)

**Priority after rebuttal.** Resolve the host and the stated fixture first. The retained internal +0.120791 ns hold path passed the exact declared STA corner with its mixed −40/−55 °C cell/SRAM libraries. Changing its data path cannot address external bus ordering. A new foundry/shuttle model, matching-temperature macro characterization, changed clock/constraints or a failed path would independently justify reopening internal signoff.
