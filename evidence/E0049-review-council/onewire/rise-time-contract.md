# A receiver with a different view of the same 1-Wire line

During the challenge round the electrical panel supplied a falsifier for the ideal pull-up used in the first council experiment. This follow-up asks whether that example can fail the transaction on the **production RTL** and whether a firmware-only timing change is sufficient in the chosen model. The preceding nominal result, simulator, 20 ns clock and instruction length are fixed.

Use one lumped pull-up at 3.3 V, 4.7 kΩ and 100 or 1,000 pF. Assume ideal low assertion, zero leakage and a receiver that changes high at the declared threshold. Round each upward crossing time `-RC ln(1−Vth/3.3)` up to a 20 ns tick. Set the target threshold to the DS18B20 guaranteed-high input of 2.2 V with local power or 3.0 V with parasitic power. These are possible modeled decision points. Set the controller threshold to an illustrative 2.31 V. A particular pad or sensor may switch earlier. Use measured commanded edges from the original run as a cross-check. The two controllers' aligned commanded pulses agree there, but this follow-up runs the ASIC only.

The peer starts presence 15 or 60 µs after **its** rising reset threshold and holds low for 60 or 240 µs. It samples each written bit 15 or 60 µs after its falling edge. It answers the final power query only if it actually decoded `CC B4`. Log presence, both decoded wire bytes, terminal marker and timing. A plausible local-power marker after a wrong command is a failed transaction. Sample the shared line through the controller threshold with its existing synchronizer. No strong pull-up, temperature conversion, transmission-line model, jitter or fitted device measurement enters the screen. [DS18B20 Rev. 6, DC/AC characteristics and 1-Wire signaling](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ds18b20.pdf)

Cross both loads, power modes, presence starts/widths and write-sampling extremes for each image. Expect the original to work at 100 pF and local-power 1,000 pF. At 1,000 pF with parasitic power, late presence can defeat the 70 µs check, and an early sample can misread a write-one released after 6 µs. Keep both failure mechanisms.

Keep the same instructions and use three compensated constant changes for a candidate. Sample presence 3 µs later, shorten write-one low from 6 to 2 µs and hold the final read observation at its original time despite the earlier release. Preserve the 70 µs slot and start-to-finish command schedule. Accept this candidate only if every in-envelope case decodes both commands and returns the right power result within one clock of its original successful completion. Probe at least one bigger, deliberately out-of-envelope load. A repeatable fault there limits the conclusion. Budget one Icarus run series, under 4 MiB scratch, finishing by 16:10 UTC. No change to the released example set.
