# Powered-board handover under cross-examination

The initial review identified a powered-host sequence that passes sampled loading and execution checks on unchanged release programs. Panel B challenged its electrical handover. Panel A challenged the scope and priority of the proposed follow-up. The fresh test below addresses the interval between accepted clock edges.

## What the parked-clock check missed

The external selector `E7` is binary `11100111`; the actual loader read decode is `ui[7:6] == 2'b10`. During an unsequenced transition to zero, bit 6 falling first can enable all eight ASIC-side data drivers. Parking the clock prevents sampled writes or instructions. Output ownership changes combinationally. The Tiny Tapeout SDK exposes the MCU's own UIO direction, and a board operator can sense the shared conductor. That interface supplies no separate telemetry for the chip's internal output-enable vector. [Qualified top-level decoder](https://github.com/KG-khangelani/protocol-emulator-asic/blob/5bdc0e6b6449a829a5da1f8f9a8060257e974843/src/project.v#L119) · [Tiny Tapeout ETR, bidirectional pin direction](https://tinytapeout.com/guides/get-started-demoboard-etr/#uio_oe_pico)

**Calculation.** E7 has six set bits. Clearing them in a strict order gives 6! = 720 possibilities. For bit 6 clearing before bit 7, a gap of d steps contributes (6 − d) × 4! orders. Counts for one through five read intervals are 120, 96, 72, 48 and 24. They sum to 360 orders and 840 intermediate read states. In the other 360, the loader read decode never appears. If bit 7 alone clears first, all 5! = 120 possible tails stay in runtime mode. These are complete counts in a specific model of transition order. No distribution or probability of actual MCU pad ordering was measured.

**Fresh check, 14:57:18–14:57:19 UTC.** New Icarus compilations exercised the release RTL and the full submitted G4 gate netlist extracted from its original archive. The runner verified the archive and netlist digests, all five RTL files and both vendor RAM models, and the three original functional cell/I/O model hashes. A one-word HALT program was loaded and read back. The chip and host released the shared pins throughout the initial park. The project remained selected and the testbench allowed no clock edge during the transitions. Each intermediate vector was held for a 2 ns simulation dwell. For each of the 720 paths it tried a released peer, a peer driving pin 6 high and a peer driving pin 6 low. It also tested the explicit two-stage E7 → 67 → 00 sequence.

## Arguments and revisions

**Panel B to C.** The RP2350, external connector and ASIC share bidirectional conductors. Which check proves an external peer has released them, and does the initial estimate cover a live peer, both RTL simulators, gate tests and a deterministic UART start?

**C's answer.** Readback can establish interface identity, actual sensed bytes and a known digital terminal observation. It cannot establish internal chip OE, absence of equal-level simultaneous drives or a guaranteed setup/hold interval. The ownership case requires isolation controlled by the host or another independently specified means of making the partner quiescent before any write or read. Finish disabling the MCU drivers before selecting a read; choose and settle a released loader-write selector before enabling MCU drivers. Disable the MCU again before host-to-chip handover and reads. During E7, restore the declared idle partner while both chip and host release UIO; supply both warm-up clocks. With the project clock then held low, clear only `ui[7]`, settle at 67, then select 00 and start execution. A real implementation must time the **settling** of each stage; issuing two software writes establishes their order at the MCU, with the physical propagation budget still to be measured. External dual-polarity probes, a calibrated current/voltage capture and an independently controlled peer may resolve different aspects of ownership. The actual fixture and pin map determine what can be checked.

I **revise the effort estimate**. Four to eight engineering hours cover an additive profile, its interpreter and a fail-fast digital smoke run at this scope. Allow 1–2 engineer-days for complete applicable peer-driven G3/G5 cases in two simulators and on the retained netlist, including power-cycle/reload and deliberately wrong ordering. Original macro-only `ena=0` pause controls remain explicitly identified. A physical ownership and UART service/start exercise needs a separate 1–2 engineer-days if the MCU, breakout, analyzer and target are already available. These are staffing estimates. Measured historical gates and the short council runs are separately recorded.

**Panel A to C.** Does digital success license a board recipe, and should a complete board campaign outrank a small architecture comparator?

**C's answer.** Digital evidence supports specifying a candidate for a powered host. It identifies the accessible signals, exact program/byte identity, a failing naive path and the ordered path that survived both the structural count and settled netlist check. A host can control its own clock, output-enable and isolation commands. To bound missed clock edges, jitter, external-partner behavior and actual pin settling it needs a named fixture, clock observation against an independent timebase, and an auditable isolation contract. A pre-run pad sample has limited evidential value for these properties. Keep the digital fail-fast gate first because the documented default plan cannot be issued as written on the powered interface. **After that gate**, a small, matched substrate comparator can take precedence over a complete board/UART campaign if the fixture or target is absent, funds are unavailable, or the immediate research decision concerns cost of the substrate. Effort on an already frozen candidate should follow the workload and claim it could change.

**Dissent that remains.** The timing of a full FPGA/board study versus architecture comparison depends on the intended external peer and available fixture. An adequately isolated physical setup, a bounded clock/start sequence and a credible named use case would promote board work. A verified counterexample on a staged path, an unpredictable live peer that cannot be isolated, or failure to meet the declared UART first-cache bound would lower its feasibility. A matched architecture result that saves physical cost without losing the required workloads would promote the comparator. Neither a high-level feasibility argument nor agreement among reviewers supplies any of those observations.
