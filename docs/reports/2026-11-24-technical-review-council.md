24 November 2026 · Review baseline `e90cca228490423a7fe7c4ae40f7e669200b4d0e` · IHP 130 nm CMOS5L, Tiny Tapeout 8×4

## What the project has demonstrated

The project has one reprogrammable, single-stream GPIO timing engine. Interface 0x22 exposes 128 × 32-bit logical instructions, two transmit bytes and one completed receive byte. A real single-port 256 × 32 SRAM supplies instruction storage. Next-PC prefetch and a word-zero shadow preserve the established edge schedule. Firmware supplies protocol framing, direction and cleanup. At G2 the complete design passed nine comparison workloads, including four protocol workloads and five primitive/reload workloads. G3 shipped a versioned assembler, listings, load/readback plans and thirteen examples. G4 published the physical candidate as **v0.1.0**. G5 added **v0.1.1**, a software release demonstrating reproducible triggered waveforms and a controlled two-clock timing fault. The owner's independent M0 explanation under G6 is still pending. **Manufacturability depends on exactly what passed.** The earlier timed-GPIO design saved 13.1456% routed standard-cell area and failed a fanout limit of eight with eleven loads. A 255-word register design reached 111.564% adjusted placement utilization; a smaller register alternative still failed global routing. The matched SRAM experiment reduced mapped cell-plus-store area from 477,788.26 to 102,578.26 µm². In the final GDS, twelve DigiBnd contact enclosures measure 60 nm against 50 nm in the guide, and twenty SRAM sites measure 10 nm against the pinned deck's 6 nm. The public SRAM guidance was unfinished. A 15 nm requirement would reject those twenty sites. Fifty-four existing full-height supply strips were verified in both geometry and exported ports. Reopen the physical candidate if the foundry supplies a stricter applicable rule, timing model or carrier constraint. The earlier failed layouts and the current official passes are both part of that decision. ## Where the current design changes behavior

**A selected board needs a different loading sequence.** The organizer says deselection powers down the chip and loses state. The released macro-port plans use low-enable clocks. On the qualified logic, simply forcing enable high advanced the program early. Two clocks at reserved selector `E7`, followed by `E7 → 67 → 00` with the clock held low, resolved that digital discrepancy. The submitted G4 netlist matched all 55 original G3 cases including 15 expected rejections, and its 1,160 I²C case file matched byte for byte. Both the submitted netlist and Verilator RTL reproduced all 1,262 runtime rows of the twenty applicable G5 cases. The twenty-first case explicitly pauses with enable low. [Powered launch, original failure and case files](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/powered-netlist/README.md)

That result exposes a separate hardware integration task. On the inspected ETR predecessor, UIO1 is shared with the RP2350 and the external connector. A UART transmitter idles actively high; the loader can drive the same pin low during readback. Among 720 possible monotone orders for changing the six selector bits, 360 passed through a loader read. All 120 orders clearing bit 7 first kept the tested route released. Those are enumerated digital paths, with no probability assigned to a real board. Clock ownership, break-before-make data direction, peer isolation during readback and a bounded first start need an actual controller. A warm rearm reduced clocked host work from 802 to eight steps in a measured I²C case, but an image of equal length and different content produced the same public end markers. Use warm rearm only after exact readback and exclusive custody since then. [Carrier wiring](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-b/etr-fixture.md) · [Selector enumeration and warm-rearm failures](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-c/README.md)

**Correct bytes can conceal a timing failure.** For the two-frame UART receiver, an exact-rate host with poll interval H and read latency L must satisfy H + L ≤ 4,340 core clocks. With a +2% faster ideal transmitter, the derived bound under the tested framing and rearm assumptions tightens to 4,254 clocks, or 85.08 µs. Eight faster-transmitter cases duplicated the second byte at the older service boundary even though the receive waveform remained good. For SPI, a previously archived exact-edge firmware profile produced raw-valid 22 clocks (440 ns) earlier than the released held-response profiles at the cost of one instruction and a changed terminal PC. Applicability depends on the peer's response envelope and the host's success signature. [UART limits and executable analysis](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-a/uart/declaration.md) · [SPI public trace comparisons](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-a/replay-guide.md)

For I²C, the original combined transaction has a shortest commanded SCL-fall to SDA-fall gap of 20 ns. NXP defines ordering at receiver voltage thresholds. One deliberately slow-fall model missed the order by 113.68 ns. A compensated 24-clock guard grew the image from 96 to 108 words while keeping the 402.92 µs transaction duration; the 159-case digital screen passed its expected outcomes. No pad/load violation was measured. For 1-Wire, the 85-word DS18B20 power query initially passed all eight ideal-wire responses. A target counting transitions at its own 3.0 V threshold with a 4.7 kΩ/1 nF pull-up sometimes saw only nine of seventeen slots. A plausible HALT and power marker concealed the malformed commands. Expanding slots to 76 µs gave 32/32 successes at the two modeled loads and still rejected two 2.2 nF stress cases. These are testable operating hypotheses for a named bus. ## Alternatives and what they cost

| Alternative | Evidence and tradeoff | Decision it can inform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Dedicated controller** | A fixed 1-Wire circuit reproduced the twelve ideal-pin dispositions of the selected transaction with 34 sequential bits and 349 pre-ABC primitives. The first full generic ABC run timed out at 120 s. The fallback count excludes a reusable instruction store, loader and the other roles. An implemented circuit gives one achievable cost; only a proved optimum of an appropriately relaxed problem would supply a lower bound. [Fixed-controller contract, failure and replay](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-a/replay-guide.md) | A useful waveform reference and design baseline. A collection of fixed controllers cannot load a newly conceived protocol. |
| **RP2040 PIO** | One 32 × 16-bit SM program plus four preload bytes handled the 1-Wire query autonomously. The VM used 85 × 32 bits. PIO consumed a whole shared instruction bank and an additional completion pin in this harness. Its 115 cold register operations and the VM's 712 clocked setup steps use different host interfaces. The vendor's shorter 17-word example delegates steps to host code. On a separate task, one 30-word PIO image ran eight consecutive SPI mode/order settings without reloading instruction memory. Of 536 single launches, 464 met the desired behavior and 72 detected the intended violation. Synchronized FIFO-ready at sample edge S+3 follows the VM exact-capture image's S+2. The two also arm pins differently before the first scored edge. [PIO and host accounting](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-a/pio-spi/README.md) · [Prelaunch reanalysis](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-a/pio-spi/startup-comparison.json) · [Official PIO program](https://github.com/raspberrypi/pico-examples/blob/0d62f75bafc2c8120d3276c3343d1a9195e909e9/pio/onewire/onewire_library/onewire_library.pio) | A credible programmable competitor on two workloads. Byte count, shared instruction slots and setup operations do not yield common-process area, elapsed setup time or energy. |
| **VM with timed UART burst disabled** | Two manual programs on production RTL preserved the checked UART wire/terminal times over 1,100 released/manual cases. TX grew from 8 to 17 words and RX from 8 to 18. A correctly completed manual RX returned B1 instead of A7 and the released host reader rejected it. On a separate full-top hardware copy, disabling the burst saved 2,130.03 µm². Logic fell 4.7633%, total nominal cell-plus-SRAM area 2.2610%, and both versions used 353 sequential cells. The two old binaries then faulted. A clean rerun reproduced both maps, 550 manual cases, four original faults and fifty other G3 case records. [Ablation, maps and reproduction](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/uart-ablation/README.md) | A measured marginal price for one instruction under one mapper. The published interface, physical route and unknown value of a tile change make removing it a poor delivery trade today. |
| **Smaller instruction representations** | A per-program literal dictionary round-tripped every original binary. Across fifteen programs, 1,324 bytes became 922. The three I²C images alone saved 436 bytes; the other twelve together grew 34 bytes. A valid all-distinct 128-word image expanded from 512 to 628 bytes. On-chip use would require constant-time indexed fetch, including straddling indices, and complete public readback. [Corpus, held-out programs and controls](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/storage-screen/README.md) | Distribution compression can favor a workload family. The installed macro size stays fixed. A shorter archive supplies no physical saving. |
| **CPU, timing peripheral or multiple engines** | TI PRU instruction timing depends on which memory/port is accessed. SERV's bit-serial instruction paths consume enough 32-clock stages that a software store per 25-clock SPI half-period is already a poor fit at the same 50 MHz. NXP FlexIO and OpenTitan illustrate shifter/timer and transaction-segment alternatives; peer competition sources show real FIFOs and pin arbitration. None received a matched complete-chip route here. [Primary documents and pinned peer sources](../research/council-2026-11-24/sources.md) | Consider a FIFO, second event path, native peripheral or extra engine only for a named service, concurrency or abort requirement. Charge the loader, storage, ownership logic, clock and software work to each candidate. |

**An external requester distinguishes architectures more sharply than another periodic master.** On unchanged RTL, a 59-word mode-0 target returned `A4 → 3C` and `A5 → A7`, rejecting 254 other commands. Across 288 RTL and eight selected submitted-netlist tests, an aborted reply remained driven for **1,563 ns** against a synthetic 160 ns limit. In 405 timeout trials, healthy 0.5 MHz operation needed operands 48–80 while active abort needed 2–9. The tested single-timeout choice has no overlap. One cooperative polling program assembled to 215 words, above 128. A CS-only buffer drove during command bits. A bilateral switch isolated MISO while CS was high but reconnected stale data and failed the next command. A program that observes only SCK during a wait cannot distinguish two input histories that differ only on CS until it observes another event. Different programs and additional hardware can implement that observation. [VM target and timeout sweep](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-c/spi-target/README.md) · [Four connection topologies](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/panel-b/fixture/README.md)

One 19-word PIO **one-bit echo** released MISO after a stopped-low abort and answered an immediate opposite-bit request. In 480 three-frame sequences, four reached exactly 160 ns. Two stopped-high aborts took 700 and 718 ns. Its failed 25-word predecessor missed normal release. These tests cover less behavior than the full two-command VM target and leave zero worst-case digital margin on the favorable clock polarity. Neither design has earned a general 160 ns claim. A full comparison must include both clock polarities, command-phase high impedance, watchdog, invalid commands, eight-bit replies, reselection and all host or external assistance.
