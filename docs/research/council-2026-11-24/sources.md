Access date was 24 November 2026. When a source describes a different chip, tool or package, the evidence applies to that implementation. Repository links below are relative to this review. Published source pins and section references let a reader reconstruct the comparison after upstream changes.

Two alternatives require particular care over the comparison unit. Fixed-width native instructions, a per-image transport dictionary and mixed 16/32-bit native encodings impose different alignment and fetch requirements. RISC-V's specification documents its own single-instruction expansions and halfword alignment. We measured the present binary corpus using a separate lossless dictionary. The [corpus measurements](../../../evidence/E0049-review-council/storage-screen/README.md) concern those released bytes. [RISC-V unprivileged ISA, 2024-04-11, C extension §25.1](https://docs.riscv.org/reference/isa/v20240411/unpriv/c-st-ext.html)

The stretch protocols change the time and electrical budget. At a 50 MHz core clock a 10 Mbit/s bit lasts five clocks and its ideal Manchester half-bit lasts 2.5. TI's 10BASE-T PHY describes its encoder, analog filtering and transformer interface. USB's 1.5 Mbit/s rate gives 33⅓ core clocks per bit before considering bit stuffing, edges and turnaround. USB-IF documents NRZI and stuffing. These calculations motivate a separate line-coding and physical-interface experiment if a stretch task is chosen. They predict neither a passing implementation nor impossibility. [TI DP83848-EP Rev. E, §5.5.1.3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/dp83848-ep.pdf) · [USB-IF bus signaling FAQ](https://www.usb.org/node/214) · [USB-IF bandwidth analysis](https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/bwpaper2.pdf)
