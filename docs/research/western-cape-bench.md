**First purchase.** Two Stellenbosch sensors, the Communica Pico and three pull-ups total **R154.15** before cables, headers, wiring or travel. The Pico can check the sensors and act as a separate UART/SPI peer. A locally accessible FPGA is required to put the project itself on that bench. The remote TT board has a different RP2350B wiring map. Its GPIO numbers must be remapped for a standalone Pico.

For the one-device-at-a-time 1-Wire trial, use the same 3.3 V data pull-up and short wiring in both runs. Supply one sensor externally and tie VDD to ground on the parasite-powered device. Decode `CC B4` least-significant bit first and the seventeenth slot at the sensor. The expected read bit is one for external power and zero for parasite power. The query does not start a temperature conversion. [Analog Devices data sheet](https://www.analog.com/media/en/technical-documentation/data-sheets/ds18b20.pdf) · [Council's receiver test](../../evidence/E0049-review-council/onewire/rise/README.md)

**Instrument quote.** Ask for simultaneous acquisition on the loaded receiver branch, probe capacitance and bandwidth, interchannel skew/deskew, acquisition rate with all channels active, calibration dates and uncertainty. For SPI and UART also specify a switchable, current-limited bias and an actual way to distinguish an actively driven high or low from a released output under either bias. Budget the scope and probes together. The existing 424 ns SPI case has only 8 ns of digital clearance on the sample grid. The 480 ns case is a more forgiving first measurement. [SPI branch method](../../evidence/E0057-remote-spi/README.md#next-electrical-test)

For I²C, include the probes in the bus load. With an ideal 4.7 kΩ pull-up the 30%–70% rise time is `0.8473 × R × C`. At 100 pF this gives 398 ns. At about 251 pF it reaches the 1 µs Standard-mode limit. Actual pin and cable loads, threshold crossings and resistor values must be measured for the fixture. [NXP UM10204, sections 6 and 7](https://www.nxp.com/docs/en/user-guide/UM10204.pdf)
