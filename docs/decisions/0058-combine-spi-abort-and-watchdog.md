# D58 — Fit SPI abort and a selected-clock watchdog in the released VM

25 November 2026. Extend the firmware experiment from [E0055](../../evidence/E0055-spi-target-comparison/README.md). The 120-word program serviced all 384 reselections and missed every selected-clock pause. Try to assemble a successor with at most 128 words using the published instruction set. Keep the six existing pins, host-free execution and the terminal-marker interface. Use the actual G4 release assembler, the public powered loader, the original requester and both released hardware models. Keep production sources, releases and earlier evidence at their qualified identities.

Score all 736 original schedules, including 384 fresh replies after abort, 32 selected-clock stalls, 32 held aborts, 254 prompt invalid-command rejections, the ordinary transfers and named faults. Require MISO release by 160 ns after abort, an uninterrupted quiet fresh command, the correct complete reply, and a stable released timeout terminal within 2 µs after a missing clock while selected. Require 60 ns setup and 40 ns hold at the requester and the existing normal release deadline. Keep marker pins released until terminal reporting. Reject any dependence on the dummy MOSI byte or another driven or biased spare pin.

Investigate counted CS/SCK polling and sharing code between response positions using the level already driven on MISO. Verify the driven bit at the public port and charge the synchronizer delay of that readback. Stress additional clock phases, high/low widths, dummy data and timeout boundaries. Retain the first failing attempt and use mutations to test the scorer. Accept a digital result after replay through qualified RTL and the submitted functional netlist. Electrical timing at a receiver and adoption into a release require their own evidence.
